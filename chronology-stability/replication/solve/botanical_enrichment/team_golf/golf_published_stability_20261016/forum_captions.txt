Ordering sensitivity
Each horizontal strip is one observed text unit or pooled bifolium. Color intensity records how often its fitted position lands in that interval. Starts and penalties have equal design weight regardless of fit quality; the report separately compares the best recovered scores. Separate dark areas preserve competing positions. These are empirical sensitivity frequencies, not historical probabilities.

Ordering sensitivity from an ElPiGraph ensemble of randomly resampled principal curves (Albergante et al. 2020, section 2.10 and Figure 1C; https://doi.org/10.3390/e22030296). Each observation has a 90% retention chance in each of 100 independently drawn masks. Eight initializations are fitted at each of two edge penalties, lambda=0.01 and 0.05, with mu=0.1 fixed. The main figure samples individual text units in the left panel and pooled physical bifolia in the right panel. Every model is refitted; native positive-MDS geometry and initial anchors remain fixed.

Each horizontal strip is one observation, grouped by its native Golf label. The strip shows its retained-fit frequencies across 100 fixed position bins, combining both penalties and all eight starts with equal weight. Darker means more fitted positions in that bin; no Gaussian approximation or smoothing is applied, so separated modes remain visible. Endpoint projection can create concentrated bins at 0 or 1. The common opacity scale saturates at 20% per bin. Rows within groups follow source order, not the inferred chronology.

The same 100 masks are reused across starts and penalties; 1600 fitted models per panel are not 1600 independent resamples. Enriched start sets differ by cohort and penalty, so these are overall model-and-sampling sensitivity frequencies, not an isolated penalty effect, posterior probabilities, or historical confidence intervals. Models receive equal weight regardless of fit quality; the separate ensemble_competition.json reports recovered objective differences. The paired physical-sheet sampling control is supplied separately.

H position and the Herbal ordering

H:58–65 and the Herbal order, using Cassidy's derivative-based continuation (2023, equations 7–8). Red favors 3a before 3b; blue favors 3b before 3a. Color measures the relative penalized-fit advantage among ten starting curve families and three checked paths to each grid location, restricted to outcomes retaining 1a before 1b. The line approximates equal recovered scores. Hatching marks locations where the three paths disagree about the preferred Herbal order. The star is the observed pooled H:58–65 profile; small dots retain the Golf colors.

The predictor uses the objective Hessian and mixed data derivative. It is verified against finite differences, corrected at explicitly detected nearest-node assignment crossings, and checked using reverse paths. This is a piecewise application of the published smooth method; crossings are not treated as smooth continuation.

Only H's displayed coordinates change, with its other 47 coordinates and the other 49 pools fixed; lambda=0.01, mu=0.1, and 28-node chain topology are fixed. These are conditional coordinate experiments, not recovered missing text, chronological probabilities or proof that all competing solutions were found. Moving along a parameter path is a numerical experiment, not manuscript time.

The native-H cell uses the original ten families with no excursion. A separate check of 36 selected excursions and returns finds additional fitted shapes: its best Herbal preference stays unchanged, while the Stars-before-Bio advantage narrows. Neither this check nor the map exhausts all possible continuation paths. Method: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-023-01200-0 .

scEGRET continuity of the original Golf route
The original scEGRET R calculation measures changes in transition features between position bins. Larger values mark sharper changes. Original bin numbers, occupancy and Golf composition remain visible, including bins too sparse for the calculation. A jump does not uniquely identify missing bifolia.

Original Golf ordering examined with the original scEGRET R continuity calculation (Zheng et al., https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbad356). The pyramid compares every eligible pair of original pseudotime bins using word-transition profiles. A diamond centered between bins a and b measures their relative feature change; greater height means a wider separation between the compared bins. The stacked bars give each original bin's observation count and Golf composition. The lower panels show word- and line-transition changes between neighboring retained bins.

The plot uses per-million transition profiles and the middle resolutions declared before inspecting results: 12 bins for individual text units and 8 for pooled bifolia. Original scEGRET takes unweighted means within bins, so the page and pooled representations do not have identical physical-sheet weighting. All other tested resolutions, raw-count controls and B120 candidate comparisons are retained in scegret_summary.json. No binning was chosen to favor an order.

Original bin numbers are never compressed. An asterisk marks bins containing f58r/v or the pooled H:58–65 profile. Bins with at most three observations are excluded by the source's strict count rule; their pair tiles are hatched, and comparisons spanning excluded bins are dashed. An excluded pseudotime bin is not evidence of a physically missing manuscript section. The summary gives retained/excluded coverage for every Golf group and the H observations, and compares both sides of the H bin with its direct neighbor-to-neighbor bypass where all three original bins are retained. These are bin-average comparisons, not an H-only effect.

A bin mixing Bio4a and Stars4c cannot determine their internal order. Pooled Stars4c has only two native observations, so a bin containing only that group would fail the minimum-count rule; a lower score after excluding it is not evidence for its chronology. Larger feature changes flag discontinuities for investigation, not an incorrect historical order or a biological state. Mean and maximum step-change comparisons in the summary are descriptive aggregations, not a published chronology-selection test.

Physical-sheet sampling control
Physical-sheet sampling control for ordering_ensemble.png. The page panel retains or omits whole physical bifolia together; its masks are matched to the pooled-bifolium panel. This separates the effect of sampling unit from the native page-versus-pool representation. All other figure conventions are unchanged: 100 masks, 90% retention chance per sheet, eight starts per penalty, lambda=0.01/0.05, retained-only positions, and a fixed common frequency scale. Individual-page Golf labels and pooled majority labels differ on mixed-label bifolia. Frequencies summarize the declared perturbations, not historical probabilities.
