What a consensus sequence means

A shifted distribution is a useful clue, but the paired comparisons are the ordering evidence: they count how often two retained observations change places in the same fit. Overlapping marginal distributions can still have a consistent internal order.

The consensus display sorts each observation by its average pairwise “after” frequency, counting a tied projection as half. It is a descriptive ranking, not a maximum-likelihood chronology or a newly fitted path. A low-support adjacent comparison marks an unresolved ordering. Pairwise preferences can form cycles, and this sorted list need not occur as one complete fitted route. Equal scores have no inferred internal order.

“Best per sample” selects the lowest-objective recovered start within each sample and λ, among fitted outcomes whose all-reference group medians put 1a before 1b. This matches the published competition analysis. It does not compare energies across representations, penalties or different samples; it does not establish that the winning route is historically correct.

Neither a strong pairwise preference nor adjacency in the list establishes a direct connection, excludes missing pages, or rules out a branch. Pages with the same closest-curve position remain tied; the display does not invent an order between them. Page labels and pooled-bifolium labels can differ on mixed sheets.

Data and calculation

No new models were fitted. Histograms exactly reproduce the published all-start charts. Pair counts use only jointly retained observations, with position differences within 10⁻⁷ counted as tied. Selected representation/penalty settings receive equal weight after normalizing their own counts. Starts, settings and transliterations share resampling masks; “joint fits” is a computational count, not a count of independent samples. Both transcription and glyph grouping differ between RF and ZL.
